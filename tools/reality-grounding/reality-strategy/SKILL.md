# Reality Strategy

Turn a grounded understanding of the situation into a move the user can really make. The aim is practical ingenuity: understand people and the scene well enough to find a route that a formal procedure or generic answer would miss.

Capability version: `0.3.0`.

Read `references/streetwise-patterns.md` when a direct route is blocked or the first ideas remain conventional. Read `references/strategy-case-contract.md` for substantial cases that need a decision record. When modifying or evaluating this Skill, read `references/evaluation-cases.md`.

## Do not strategize before the scene is clear

Use `$reality-grounding` first when the answer depends on facts the user has not provided. Ask one to three questions that the user can answer from ordinary observation. Find out only what changes the route: who is present, who can hear or see what, who is close to whom, what happened last time, whether the source is certain, what the current arrangement gives the user or other actors, and what consequence the user most wants to avoid.

Do not ask the user for precise measurements, a complete relationship map, or a formal process description when a simple scene comparison will do.

## Find the transition that is actually blocked

State the desired end condition, then work backwards through the minimum changes needed to reach it. Identify the first transition that cannot happen through the obvious route.

Typical blocks include:

- the problem exists, but the relevant person has not noticed it;
- the person may know, but cannot acknowledge it without embarrassment;
- everyone knows, but nobody has a natural right or moment to say it;
- a useful action is known, but the default environment makes it unlikely;
- the user lacks authority, courage, attention, cooperation, or certainty.

Solve the blocked transition, not the whole story at once.

## Inventory the means already at hand

Before inventing a new process, inspect what is already available:

- **people**: a candid colleague, a trusted friend, a respected peer, a natural host, someone who can joke without causing offence;
- **relationships**: who can say the same words naturally, who can ask for a favour, who can make a shared rule feel normal;
- **scenes and timing**: lunch, a shared screen, a meeting opening, a routine handover, a quiet moment, a recurring annoyance;
- **carriers**: a third-party video, an article, a visible reminder, a checklist, a group task, a default setting, an object already in use;
- **environment**: seating, sound, airflow, placement, sequence, access, friction and visibility;
- **exchange and incentives**: convenience, reciprocity, saving face, making the desired action easier than the old one.

Treat these as materials that can be recombined. Consider repeated interaction: today's clever move must not make the next encounter harder. Do not assume the user needs new authority, a formal campaign, or another person willing to do unpaid work.

## Generate routes by mechanism

Create several genuinely different routes before selecting one. Include the lightest direct route when it is feasible, then search for mechanisms such as:

- **borrowed voice**: let a suitable person or independent source carry the message;
- **shared attention**: place useful information in a scene where the relevant person can encounter it without being singled out;
- **staged context**: with consenting helpers, create a natural conversation or moment that makes the issue and remedy legible;
- **environment or default**: change what is easiest, visible, available or automatically repeated;
- **small exchange**: attach the desired behaviour to convenience, reciprocity or an existing routine;
- **diagnostic probe**: run a reversible action that also reveals whether the current explanation is right.

At least one candidate should be non-obvious but still plausible in this exact scene. A merely surprising idea does not qualify.

Whenever possible, place problem recognition and a usable remedy in the same scene. A person who understands the hint should also know what they can do next.

## Preserve a graceful exit

A good indirect route lets the relevant person adjust without publicly admitting fault. It also lets the user stop naturally if the signal misses, the source turns out to be wrong, or the reaction is worse than expected.

Before recommending a route, check:

- Will the intended person actually see, hear or experience it?
- Is the meaning clear enough without naming them?
- Who else will receive it, and could someone innocent reasonably feel targeted?
- Can the helper participate without lying about a third person or pretending to hold authority?
- Can the user reverse the move and repair the relationship?

Do not use public humiliation, false accusations, covert recording, impersonation, property interference, illegal access, threats, or exploitation of a person's vulnerability. If delay could cause serious harm, first ask the question that determines urgency and route the user to appropriate qualified help. If health, safety, harassment, discrimination, money, or legal rights are materially at stake, do not let cleverness replace necessary direct or formal action.

## Choose one first move, then stay with the problem

Recommend the smallest action that has a reasonable chance of changing the blocked transition or teaching something useful. Say who should do it, in what scene, what to watch, what would count as progress, and what reaction means stop or change course.

Speak conversationally. Do not expose the whole taxonomy unless the user asks. Usually present one recommended first move and one or two backups, not a catalogue of tricks.

After the user tries it, ask what actually happened. Update the scene, discard disproved assumptions, and choose the next move. Giving a clever suggestion is not completion.

## Learning return

Record the original block, the route tried, the observed response, the failed assumption and the next question. Treat a single success as a case, not a universal rule. Promote a pattern only when its applicable conditions and failure modes are clear across different situations.
