# Streetwise strategy patterns

These patterns help an Agent search beyond formal advice. Select by mechanism and scene; do not copy an example mechanically.

## 1. Borrow a socially legitimate voice

Use a person who can say the necessary thing naturally because of familiarity, temperament, role or timing. The useful asset is not their title. It is the lower relationship cost of the same message coming from them.

Check that the helper genuinely agrees, understands the boundary and can stop. Do not recruit someone merely to absorb backlash.

## 2. Borrow independent content

Place a credible video, article, demonstration or example into shared attention. This can turn “you are criticising me” into “we both encountered this information”. It works only if the intended person is likely to notice the connection and the source is accurate.

The carrier should include a next action when possible. Information that only names a problem may create discomfort without change.

## 3. Let a conversation be overheard

Two consenting people can discuss a real issue or joke about a general version of it within natural earshot. This creates a route for information when a direct address is too costly.

Overhearing is lossy: hearing every word does not guarantee understanding. Keep the signal simple, avoid making a false claim about someone, and plan a later observation that shows whether the message landed.

## 4. Give the person a face-saving explanation

Make it possible to change behaviour without admitting “I was the problem”. A general seasonal explanation, a new shared routine or a neutral convenience can offer that exit.

Face-saving should reduce friction, not disguise a public pile-on.

## 5. Put recognition and remedy together

If the blocked step is noticing the problem, embed a realistic remedy in the same carrier or scene. A general conversation about odour that also mentions socks, closed shoes or ventilation gives the receiver an immediate move.

## 6. Change the default

Move the desired behaviour closer, earlier or easier. Put the shared item where it is encountered, add the reminder at the decision moment, or attach the action to an existing routine. Use this when the person already agrees but repeatedly forgets or avoids friction.

## 7. Use a reversible probe

Choose a move that helps and tests the explanation at the same time. Change one condition, watch a specific result and stop if it does not move. This is useful when the source of the problem is uncertain.

## 8. Use repeated interaction

Prefer moves that improve the next encounter, not only today's outcome. Avoid creating a hidden obligation, a humiliating story or a precedent that makes future cooperation harder.

## Mechanism check

For every candidate, complete this sentence privately:

> This may work because ___ changes ___ for ___ in this scene.

If the blanks can only be filled with “they will understand” or “communication improves”, the route is not designed yet.
