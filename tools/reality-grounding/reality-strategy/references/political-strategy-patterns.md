# Political strategy patterns for ordinary life and organisations

Use these patterns when another person controls the outcome and ordinary advice only improves the wording of a request. The task is to understand the small political system around the problem: interests, vetoes, audiences, status, ownership, risk and the next move.

## Find the outcome owner and the quiet veto

Separate the person who benefits from the status quo, the person who pays its cost, the person who can change it, and the person who can quietly block change. These may be four different people.

## Write the status-quo payoff

Complete this sentence privately:

> They may keep doing ___ because it gives them ___, while ___ bears most of the cost.

If the only explanation is “they do not understand”, keep investigating. Lack of understanding is possible, but it should not be used to hide a stable incentive.

## Change the person's own calculation

Possible levers include:

- make a genuine but ignored cost, risk or responsibility visible;
- return a recurring burden to the person who creates or controls it;
- make the desired behaviour easier, safer, more prestigious or more convenient;
- make the old behaviour require monitoring, explanation or repeated effort;
- give another actor a real reason to support the same result;
- protect an asset, relationship, reputation or source of control the person already values.

Never invent an incident, complaint or authority order.

## Let the decision-maker own the exit

Where possible, let the person notice the issue, propose the adjustment, take credit or explain it using a value they already hold. The public explanation may differ from the user's private reason, but it must survive later scrutiny.

## Choose audience, timing and sequence

The same message can feel like help in private and defeat in public. Decide who needs to see it, who must not be present, whose reaction matters, and which stakeholder should be approached first.

## Build a small coalition without creating a mob

Prefer helpers whose interests already overlap with the result. A person with their own reason to help is more reliable than someone recruited only to absorb backlash. Keep the coalition as small as possible.

## Shape the field before the disputed moment

Change scheduling, placement, access, defaults, preparation, measurement or who is present before entering an argument. Stay within the user's permission and keep the move safe and reversible.

## Search ladder

Move down this ladder until at least one feasible route no longer depends on winning agreement:

1. clearer words;
2. better messenger;
3. better scene and audience;
4. changed default or friction;
5. changed ownership of the burden;
6. changed risk, reward, status or control;
7. changed alliance or sequence of decisions;
8. the outcome owner discovers, authors or claims the exit.

Only show the user the few routes that survive the reality and safety checks.
