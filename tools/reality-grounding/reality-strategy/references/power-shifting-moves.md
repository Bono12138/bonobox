# Moves that change the position

Use this reference when a proposed answer sounds reasonable but the user remains dependent on the same response, permission or goodwill.

## Initiative

Convert a missing contribution into a concrete object the user is authorised to create: a draft, prototype, filled template, proposed schedule, bounded options or working assumption. Ask others to correct something specific instead of inventing the whole answer.

## Default

State what will happen if nobody acts. A useful default permits bounded progress. It is valid only when the user has authority, the action is reversible or low-risk, and the recipient receives notice and a genuine chance to object.

## Burden

Move the burden from repeated chasing to a specific objection. Require the objector to identify the wrong part and provide a correction, owner or workable alternative. Do not demand proof that the person cannot reasonably obtain.

## Cost ownership

Reconnect exceptions and delay to the actor who creates them. The person requesting an exception may need to coordinate it; the person withholding a decision may need to own the resulting branch or date; the person using another's expertise may need to provide visibility, access or reciprocal value.

## Coalition

Give downstream users, adjacent owners or sponsors a concrete object and a decision that matters to their own work. A coalition is people with aligned reasons to act, not a long CC list.

## Sequence

Change which decision happens first. Secure an accepted working version, a small pilot, a review slot, a named owner or a precedent before asking for the larger contested change.

## Alternative

Reduce dependence by developing another source, route, skill, supplier, dataset, venue or decision path. An alternative can change negotiation even before it is used, but do not bluff about one that does not exist.

## Durable gain

Prefer moves that leave behind a reusable artefact, verified fact, named owner, accepted default, precedent, new access, trained ally or credible alternative. A conversation that leaves no changed state is easy for the old arrangement to absorb.

## Attack-defence check

For each candidate, answer:

1. What can the user do after this move without asking the same permission again?
2. What must the other actor now do if they want to stop or change it?
3. What does silence produce?
4. Who bears delay and error now?
5. Which additional people independently benefit from continuation?
6. What survives if the immediate attempt fails?

If the answers are “nothing”, “nothing”, “more waiting”, “the user”, “nobody” and “an email”, discard the candidate.

## Draft-to-default pattern

When missing input keeps work frozen, the user may be able to create a labelled working version from available evidence, expose its uncertain parts, and ask the actual knowledge holders to correct specific items by a real deadline. If the user is authorised to continue and the consequences are reversible or bounded, advance the stated work under that version after the deadline. Anyone objecting must then identify the disputed part and provide a correction, decision owner or workable alternative.

This is not valid when permission or the consequence of a wrong assumption is unknown. Ask first. Where affirmative approval is required, use the working version to force a named authorised decision and decision date, or isolate a safe test that can proceed without treating silence as approval.
