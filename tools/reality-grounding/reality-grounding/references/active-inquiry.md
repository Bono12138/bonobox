# Active reality inquiry

Use this guide when the Agent must decide what to inspect or ask next.

## 1. Find the information that has decision value

Do not collect background merely because it is available. A gap deserves
attention when materially different answers change at least one of:

- whether an option is possible;
- who can act or block;
- the risk or unacceptable downside;
- the first executable step;
- whether the task should continue, stop, narrow or escalate.

Write the branches before collecting. If all plausible answers lead to the same
action, mark the gap low value and proceed without asking.

## 2. Use the strongest low-friction evidence first

Prefer this order, while respecting project permissions:

1. evidence already present in the task or conversation;
2. actual artefacts used in the last real case;
3. approved system records, queries, logs and knowledge sources;
4. direct observation of current work;
5. a small reversible probe that exposes the real constraint;
6. a targeted question to the person who directly knows or decides;
7. an explicit assumption with branches when evidence cannot be obtained.

The order is not absolute. A current system record may outrank an old report;
the actual operator may be more reliable than an obsolete procedure. Record why
the selected source can answer this gap.

## 3. Choose the next inquiry

Rank open gaps by:

- decision impact;
- chance that the answer will separate the available actions;
- reliability and freshness of the available source;
- time and effort needed;
- permission and reversibility;
- urgency of the decision.

Investigate the highest-value accessible gap first. Batch actions only when the
same source can answer them without creating extra work.

## 4. Ask a branch-changing question

Avoid broad prompts such as “please explain the process” or “provide the data
dictionary”. Ask for the smallest observable fact that separates actions.

Examples:

- “上一次这个结果真正发出去前，最后是谁改了文件？”
- “你现在能直接打开这张表，还是每次都要找数据团队导出？”
- “如果本周不处理，谁会在什么节点来追这件事？”
- “把最近一次实际使用的版本给我就够了，不需要重新整理流程。”

Do not present an interrogation. Explain why the answer matters when that helps
the user respond. Ask one to three questions, use the answers, then decide
whether another round is still necessary.

## 5. Learn from missing and conflicting evidence

Missing evidence is itself informative only within limits. It may indicate that
the process is informal, ownership is unclear or the requested object never
existed; it may also mean the Agent searched the wrong place or lacks access.
Keep these explanations separate.

When sources conflict:

- compare time, scope and directness;
- distinguish written rule from observed practice;
- identify who produced each source and for what purpose;
- seek the smallest additional observation that separates the explanations;
- preserve the conflict if it cannot be resolved before the decision.

## 6. Stop deliberately

Stop collecting when one of these is true:

- the material branch has been resolved with adequate evidence;
- the remaining gap cannot change the current action;
- the decision must proceed under a named assumption;
- the cost or delay of more collection exceeds its decision value;
- required access or authority is unavailable;
- a human choice, not more evidence, is now required.

Do not use “more research” to postpone a decision. Do not use urgency to hide a
critical unknown that could make the action unsafe or impossible.
